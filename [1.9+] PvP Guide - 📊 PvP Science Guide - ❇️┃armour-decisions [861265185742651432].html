🧠 ***__Armour Decisions__*** 🧠 **Author:** `LoCr` **Translator:** `Zivkovic` **Writer:** `iDexy` Which armour should you choose if you have an armour of a lower tier but enchanted and an armour of a higher tier but unenchanted? The answer isn't easy, first off, here are some categories: ***Category 1:*** 🔖 `Index For Comparing Armours` 🔖 To compare armours, we don't look at the reduction they give, but instead how much damage you receive compared to without armour. Here is an example **(no armour penetration in this example so its easier to understand):** 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > First, we calculate the reduction: > > Iron armour has `15 armour points`, each reduces the damage `4%` so a full set reduces `60%` > > Next, we calculate the percentage of incoming damage: > **We look at how much damage is taken:** `100%-60%=40%` > > In the last step we compare the damage taken to the damage when unarmoured, what this means is: `100%/40%=2.5` > > **2,5 is the index number for how strong the armour is ** > > Here's some examples for a better understanding: > > `90% reduction = 10` > `80% reduction (full dia) = 5` > `60% reduction (full iron) = 2.5` > `50% reduction = 2` > `33% reduction = 1.5` > `25% reduction = 1.33333` > `0% reduction = 1` You can figure out how strong your armour is compared to other ones with this index. For a better understanding, index value 1 is unarmoured, so when the index is 2 it means that you can tank 2 times as much damage compared to when unarmoured. When it's 3 it means you can tank 3 times more damage compared to unarmoured, and so on. This shows that **full dia (5) is twice as strong as full iron (5 compared to 2.5) which means you can tank 2x more damage with full dia than with full iron** `(Keep in mind this is without armour penetration)` . ***Category 2:*** 🤔 `What Can We Learn From The Index` 🤔 Because the armour reduction is a percentage, it starts off weak and gets stronger with each additional armour point. As you can see in the example above, full iron with its **15 armour points is only half as good as full diamond which only has 5 armour points more. So these last 5 points have the same protection value as the 15 points before.** This imbalance gives each new armour point a higher value **Here are some examples in the armour index:** `1 armour point = 4% reduction = index value 1.0417 --> total index increase = 0.0417` `2 armour points = 8% reduction = index value 1.087 --> total index increase = 0.0453` `3 armour points = 12% reduction = index value 1.1363 --> total index increase = 0.0493` `4 armour points = 16% reduction = index value 1.19 --> total index increase = 0.0537` `5 armour points = 20% reduction = index value 1.25 --> total index increase = 0.06` `6 armour points = 24% reduction = index value 1.3158 -->total index increase = 0.0658` `7 armour points = 28% reduction = index value 1.388 -->total index increase = 0.0722` `8 armour points = 32% reduction = index value 1.471 -->total index increase = 0.083` `9 armour points = 36% reduction = index value 1.5625 -->total index increase = 0.0915` `10 armour points = 40% reduction = index value 1.666 -->total index increase = 0.1041` (edited)

***Category 3:*** 🛡️ `The Index With Protection Enchantments And Resistance Effect` 🛡️ The index value for the basic protection enchantment works the same way, except its max is `16 points`, so an overall reduction of `64%`. The other armour enchants like blast, projectile and fire protection have their max at `20 points` which means they protect a maximum of `80%` ❗ **__IMPORTANT__** ❗ `-` Protection enchantments aren't affected by armour penetration If you want to know the armour strength, put the value into the index to compare it with unarmoured damage. Now to get the overall Index value, multiply the index value of the armour points with the index value of the protection enchantment points **Here is an example (no armour penetration in this example):** 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > Full iron armour (`15 armour points`) full protection 4 (`16 protection points`): > > Index value for `15 armour points: 100%/(1-(15x4%)) = 2.5` > > Index value for `16 protection points: 100%/(1-(16x4%))= 2.777` > > To get now the overall Index value we multiply them: > > `2.5 x 2.777 = 6.94` > > So __the Index value of full iron with protection 4 is `6.94` which means you can tank `6.94` times more damage compared to without armour__ > > The calculation for the Index value with resistance works the same way 📜 **__Special Rule__** 📜 If you have a reduction over `100%` which can be obtained with resistance 5 or higher, the index will show negative values. In that case, it's no longer representative because you can tank infinite damage . (edited)

***Category 4:*** 🗡️ `Armour Penetration` 🗡️ As you saw in **<#860547038729732096> Category 4**, armour penetration reduces armour point reduction by `2%` for each incoming hp (half heart) damage. In **Category 2** we mentioned that each additional armour point is stronger than its predecessor. Armour penetration takes away the high value of additional armour points, which leads to a huge decrease in damage reduction through armour points **Here is an example with full iron armour:** 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > **Armour Points 15 -->** `15x4% = 60%` > **Incoming Damage -->** `10 hp (5 hearts) --> 10x2%= 20% armour penetration` > **Total Armour Reduction -->** `60%-20%= 40%` > > **Armour index:** `100%/(100%-40%) = 1.6666` So, your armour gets weakened from a ` 2.5 index value to a 1.66666 index value` To get an understanding of how strong the armour penetration is, you can take a look at this example of a stone/iron/dia axe critical hit against full iron (this time no calculations just the results): > 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > > **Stone/Iron/Dia axe critical hit:** `13.5 hp (6.75 hearts) damage` > **Overall reduction after Armour Penetration:** `33%` > > **Damage Taken =** `9.045 hp (4.5225 full hearts)` > **Armour Index:** `1.4925` > > **Same example without Armour Penetration:** > > **Taken damage =** `5.4 hp (2.7 full hearts)` > Armour Index: `2.5` So the damage is` 67.5%` higher with armour penetration compared to without it . ***Category 7:*** ⭕ `Factors For Armour Decisions` ⭕ The decision of which armour to use depends on `5 factors`. As we said before, there isn't an overall solution. It depends on how high the influence from a specific factor is. **These are the 5 factors:** `1.` Each additional armour point is stronger compared to its predecessor (look at **Category 2**) `2.` Each additional protection point and resistance level is stronger compared to its predecessor `3.` The damage high, the higher the damage is the higher the armour penetration is, which decreases protection from armour points `4.` Armour toughness, which reduces armour penetration `5.` The Damage types, the protection enchantments and resistance effect protects from more types of damage than regular armour points **(<#860547038729732096> Category 3)** . (edited)

`Factors 1 and 2:` As we learned in **Category 2**, each extra point of armour and protection is more worthy compared to its predecessor. That means it’s better to gain points (armour or protection) where you already have points. Like adding protection 1 to an piece of an unenchanted armour set doesn't do much while getting an extra armour point is stronger. The same thing happens when you have a lot of protection points and less armour points but this time the protection 1 enchantment is the better option compared to an extra armour point `Factor 3:` This factor is influenced by the opponent’s attack strength. If they do little damage (fist or wood sword) it doesn't matter much. But as the incoming damage gets higher, so does the influence. As described in **Category 4** and the **<#860547038729732096> Category 4**, each incoming health point (half heart) damage reduces the armour point protection by `2%`, with a minimum of `0.8% per armour point` This leads to armour points being less important than protection enchantments What this means is that as higher the damage is, the better enchantments and the resistance effect are compared to normal armour points. Especially lower tier armours like gold, leather, chain and iron are weak due to armour penetration . (edited)

`Factor 4:` This factor is influenced by the armour toughness level of your armour since the more armour toughness you have, the less armour penetration weakens your armour This means that for low damage values the armour toughness isn’t that important, but as the damage gets higher, so does the importance of armour toughness `Factor 5:` There are some damage types armour points don’t reduce. For example, magical damage (poison or instant damage) doesn't get reduced by armour points. So, when fighting against them be aware and try to get as many basic protection enchantments as possible to get a reduction for these damage types These damage types only get reduced by the basic protection enchantment and the resistance effect, not by armour points: `-` Magical damage `-` Fall damage --> feather falling reduces this too `-` Drowning `-` Colliding with a block while flying with an elytra `-` Suffocating inside a block or due to entity cramming (also includes the world border) The only damage types which cannot be reduced at all are: `-` Starvation `-` Void damage `-` `/kill` command For more information look here **<#860547038729732096> Category 3** . ***Category 8:*** 🪙 `A Good Investment Of Resources` 🪙 First, because of what we learned from factors I and II, try to always to get upgrades to your armour when you already have a lot of armour or protection points. That means getting a leather cap while already having **`6 armour points (+1 armour point)` doesn't increase your armour strength much**, so in this case making one is a waste of time However, a **leather cap added to `3 current dia pieces (17 armour points)` increases your armour strength a lot which makes it worth it even though it may take some time to craft and gather resources** **Some Examples:** 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > **3/4 leather (without helmet) -->** `6 armour points` > **Incoming Damage -->** `6 hp (3 hearts)` > **Armour Index -->** `1.136` > > **Full leather -->** `7 armour points` > **Incoming Damage -->** `6 hp (3 hearts)` > **Armour Index -->** `1.19` > > **Index increase due to helmet upgrade:** `0.054` 📝 **__Example__** 📝 > **3/4 dia (without helmet) -->** `17 armour points` > **Incoming Damage -->** `6 hp (3 hearts)` > **Armour index -->** `2.573` > > **3/4 dia with leather helmet -->** `18 armour points` > **Incoming Damage -->** `6 hp (3 hearts)` > **Armour Index -->** `2.868` > > **Index increase due to helmet upgrade:** `0.295` **So, in which situations is this decision necessary?** In general, it’s always better to have all the armour you can get. But when you have little time like in skywars, survival games, kit 1vs1, or any game mode where time plays a big role and you are under pressure, you need to quickly decide how to use your time most efficiently . ***Category 12:*** 🔢 `Protection Points` 🔢 As explained in **<#860547038729732096> Category 7**, there are 5 different enchantments which protect you from certain damage types The basic protection enchantment gives you `1 protection point (EPF)` for each level you wear Each point reduces the damage taken by `4%` This means one piece protection `4 armour gives 16 % reduction,` and a full set of protection `4 gives a reduction of 64%` The difference between the basic protection enchantment and the specific protection enchantments is that the specific ones give you 2 protection points but only against the specified damage type. So one piece with a specific protection enchantment at level `4 gives you 32% reduction` against the damage it works against An important thing to consider is that only a maximum of `20 protection points` will be counted in the reduction calculation. But when wearing a full set of level 4 against a specific damage you have `32 protection points` against that damage. To prevent that values over `100%` reduction are possible, **the game counts only `20 protection points` and ignores any extra ones** **This means that having a full level 4 set against a specific kind of damage is stupid, because you lose 12 protection points** So, in regular PvP, players use armour sets with 1 piece enchanted against a specific damage and 3 pieces with basic protection all at level 4. So they have the maximum protection against the specific kind of damage (`20 Points`) and also have `12 protection points` against all kinds of damage . (edited)

***Category 13:*** 📖 `Quick Guide For Decisions` 📖 We have finally reached the last sub category, so here are some "rules" for how to react ingame when you have multiple armour types but with different enchantments or toughness levels ❗ **__IMPORTANT__** ❗ These "rules" work for most scenarios with a low amount ( 6 <) of protection points, which you will likely have in most gamemodes, but not for extreme situations like extremely high damage or a big difference between armour and protection points **The quick rules:** `-` If a piece gives 2 protection points more but has 1 armour point less compared to the unenchanted piece, choose the piece with the 2 protection points `-` If a piece has armour toughness and is 1 armour point better compared to the enchanted piece, you need around 4 protection points on the enchanted piece for it to be a better choice `-` If you have a low amount of armour points (10 <), protection points are more important to have. If you have a lot of armour points, protection points are less important to have . (edited)
